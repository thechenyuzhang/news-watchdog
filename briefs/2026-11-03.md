## What to read / what to watch

1. **Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang, Omar Khattab, "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming"** (Nov 2, long blog with logs, **New creator**, HN 2,877). Read it with the rebuttal below.
   - **What they did:** gave GPT-6 Astra raw weights of small networks and asked it to say what they do (named the game 4/4, recovered a diffusion model's target shape, a char-LM's language and induction heads), fix them (Kinetix car policy 0→100%), and write new ones (MinAtar nets that beat PPO-100M, a 9×9 Go net).
   - **The big claim:** on Qwen3.5-0.8B it found and removed a "looping" direction touching 131,072 weights (0.016%), lifting MATH-500 7.9→48.1%, GSM8K 13.8→67.1%, IFEval 21.0→62.3%. No gain on Gemma 3 270M.
   - **Limits they state:** it couldn't detect a sycophantic Gemma from weights alone; it could with attention patterns. Logs at github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs.

   ([rikiyat.github.io](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/))
2. **Matthew Honnibal, "The 'Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen' post is misleading"** (Nov 2, essay, **New creator**, HN 441). The spaCy creator separates the experiments that really put raw weights in context (the 17K–172K-weight ones) from the Qwen result, which used Python and ~50 forward passes, i.e. an agent doing normal interpretability work. Useful as a template for reading viral AI-capability posts. ([honnibal.dev](https://honnibal.dev/blog/astra-read-write-misleading))
3. **Core Automation (Rohan Anil), "The Hitchhiker's Guide to Training on Compute Islands"** (Nov 2, technical essay, **New creator**). How to train one model across two clusters 9,000 miles apart on a 25 Gbps link: outer/inner optimizers in the DiLoCo family, butterfly mixing across 8 cells matching baseline, a Distributed Shampoo variant, and the ~1% bits-per-byte win at equal compute. The most concrete public recipe for multi-site training this year. ([coreauto.com](https://www.coreauto.com/hitchhikers-guide-to-training-on-compute-islands))
4. **knlb, "Resisting Cognitive Surrender"** (Nov 1, essay, **New creator**, HN 256). A practitioner's playbook for staying in charge of coding agents: notice automation bias (the repeated "yes, continue"), force yourself into the loop (an AGENTS.md rule against direct file edits), a four-column PR review view, and deterministic validation, shadow traffic and style guides. Anchors: Bainbridge's *Ironies of Automation* and Andy Grove's "delegation without follow-through is abdication." ([knlb.dev](https://knlb.dev/essays/surrender))
5. **Nicholas Carlini, "Could a modern chess engine on a Pentium have defeated Kasparov in 1996?"** (Nov 1, essay, HN 189). He ported Stockfish to a 200 MHz Pentium Pro with 64 MB of RAM (~3300 Elo estimated). About +720 of the +1,335 Elo gain since 1996 is algorithmic. Modern engine on 1996 hardware vs 1996 Crafty on modern hardware: 78 wins, 17 draws, 5 losses. The port was written with LLMs. The point for AI: algorithmic headroom can matter as much as hardware. ([nicholas.carlini.com](https://nicholas.carlini.com/writing/2026/algorithmic-improvements-chess.html))
6. **Dominic Plouffe, "Survival LLM, No AI Model: 21 of 22 Titles, 9 With All Facts"** (Nov 3, 6-min test, watch-list → promoted). He tested an offline survival-QA app's library search with no model: the right title in the top 5 for 21 of 22 questions, but every key fact for only 9. He recomputed the project's judge files: the 16 GB-default Granite 4.2 8B was fully right on 3 of 22. Takeaways: loose regex facts inflate retrieval scores; retrieval is necessary but not sufficient; check content licences (several sources are non-commercial). ([dplouffe.ca](https://dplouffe.ca/2026/11/03/survival-llm-offline-ai-search-test/))
