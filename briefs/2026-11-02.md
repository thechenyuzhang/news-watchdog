## What to read / what to watch

1. **Frank Calegari (guest post on Terence Tao's blog), "Mathematics has changed. Let's act like it"** (Nov 1, essay, HN 349). A University of Chicago number theorist responds to OpenAI's October dump of 700+ math papers. He says those papers are "atrociously written" and sometimes wrong, but the tools are real. His six proposals: judge all work as if it were AI-assisted, interview candidates in person, demand more from journals, teach breadth, give better seminars, and take ownership of AI results. His key line: "Choosing worthwhile problems may end up mattering more than producing proofs." It's useful beyond math for anyone hiring in an AI-heavy field. ([Tao's blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/01/mathematics-has-changed-lets-act-like-it/))
2. **Robert Haas, "Postgres 19 is late, and it's AI's fault"** (Oct 30, essay, **New creator** in these briefs, HN 677). He compares feature-freeze to Sept 25 commit counts: master commits slightly down, back-patched commits up 60%+, and commits to the previous stable branch up 74%. LLMs can draft fixes, but humans still review and commit them, and review time was diverted from the release. His open question: how does a 40-year-old codebase catch up and still innovate? ([rhaas.blogspot.com](http://rhaas.blogspot.com/2026/10/postgres-19-is-late-and-its-ais-fault.html))
3. **Dominic Plouffe, "The AI Manager Is the Easy Part. Trusting the Code Is the Hard Part."** (Nov 1, 14-min essay, **New creator**). A CTO's practical guide to orchestrated coding agents.
   - **Thesis:** dispatching, retrying and escalating are easy (he ships a 33-line `mini_manager.py`). What's hard is a check the agent can't talk its way around, so keep tests, CI config and lint rules outside the agent's worktree.
   - **Autonomy table by blast radius:** docs merge after CI; auth and billing need human approval; migrations are proposal-only.
   - **Numbers:** 51.5% of his $22.09 pipeline spend went to verification. He suggests four metrics: time to validated delivery, rollback rate, human interventions per change, and cost per accepted change.

   ([dplouffe.ca](https://dplouffe.ca/2026/11/01/ai-coding-agents-autonomous-engineering-teams/))
4. **Simon Willison, "The U.S. Census Bureau and 'Super Intelligence'"** (Nov 2, short essay). He re-ran the Census Bureau survey (BTOS) AI-adoption chart: large firms answering "yes" went from ~30% in Feb 2026 to ~40% in Oct 2026 under the "any business function" wording. From Oct 19 the survey says "Super Intelligence (SI)" instead of AI because of Executive Order 14434, so Thursday's data may not be comparable. ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/2/census-bureau-super-intelligence/))
5. **SemiAnalysis, "GDPx: Measuring Frontier Model Capabilities Across the Most Valuable Domains"** (Nov 2, benchmark launch; the write-up is paywalled, the leaderboard is public). The argument is that the first $1T of AI revenue will come from a few high-ROI domains, so the benchmark covers chip design, cyber, bio, finance, legal, quant trading and similar fields. Each task has an 8-hour limit and runs in the model's own harness (Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity). Their QA flags ~50% of Terminal Bench 4.0 tasks as broken. ([GDPx site](https://gdpx.semianalysis.com/) · [Unite.AI summary](https://www.unite.ai/semianalysis-launches-gdpx-benchmark-spanning-11-agentic-domains/))
