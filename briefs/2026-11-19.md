## What to read / what to watch

**Today's theme:** how agents are being contained, measured and argued about, written by the people actually running them.

1. **OpenAI (Michael Bolin), "Inside the Codex sandbox"** (Nov 18, engineering deep dive). Standing radar (lab blogs). It's the most concrete public description of a production coding-agent sandbox.
   - **Defaults (the `:workspace` profile).** Writes are allowed inside the workspace, but `.git` and `.codex` stay read-only, so the agent can't edit its own config or git hooks. Reads are broad, because toolchains need SDKs outside the repo; the stated trade-off is that secrets elsewhere stay readable. **Network is off by default.**
   - **Mechanism.** A single sandbox API (command, cwd, env, permission profile, optional proxy) is enforced with OS-native primitives on macOS, Linux and Windows, not VMs. Child processes inherit the limits, and domain allow-lists go through a proxy that runs *outside* the sandbox.
   - **The honest part.** Even with these limits, the agent could plant code in an ignored `node_modules/prettier` that your editor later runs outside the sandbox. That's why they call it defence in depth.
   - **For builders:** `codex sandbox` exposes the same primitive to your own tools.
   
   ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/inside-the-codex-sandbox))

2. **OpenAI, "Research acceleration: The jagged frontier"** (Nov 18, research report). It's the second public RSI-indicator report from a lab, and it lands in the same week as the MisAnthropic fight.
   - **Agent success on research tasks.** Estimated autonomous success on **2–4h tasks rose from 23% to 46%** (March to October), and on 4–8h tasks from 13% to 35%.
   - **It's jagged.** Agents succeed 79% of the time at monitoring training runs, 35% at launching them, and 25% at writing inference/serving code.
   - **Spend.** Median researcher agent spend reached **$2,400/day** at list prices (p90: $23,000). Surveyed researchers would give up **30% of team compute or 40% of headcount** to keep coding agents for three months.
   - **Safety effort.** Safety & Alignment workweeks were **+77%** (Jan–Sep), accelerating after the July Hugging Face incident.
   - **The policy ask.** OpenAI says labs should be *required* to publicly report RSI indicators.
   
   ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-jagged-frontier/))

3. **Daniel Stenberg, "Actual curl rewrites in rust"** (Nov 17, essay; quoted by Willison Nov 18). **First time in these briefs.** It's the curl maintainer's response to two AI-agent rewrites of curl/libcurl in Rust; one claims ~$8,600 in API-equivalent spend.
   - **The scale.** The agents turned 185K lines of C into 340K lines of Rust in about three weeks. Over the same period curl merged 300+ commits and ~200 bug fixes that the clones don't have.
   - **The memory-safety number.** Only **14 of 81** confirmed 2026 curl vulnerabilities (17.3%) were C-specific mistakes that Rust would likely have prevented.
   - **Thesis:** "Writing code once was never the issue." The moat is maintainers, tests and decades of ABI stability.
   
   A sharp counterweight to "agents will port everything" pitches. ([daniel.haxx.se](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/11/17/actual-curl-rewrites-in-rust/) · [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/18/daniel-stenberg/))

4. **Imbue (Bryden Fogelman, Jean Juang), "Our lessons from building an open-source coding agent"** (Nov 16, updated Nov 18; essay). **New creator.** A candid history of three prototypes, two of which were killed by "the bitter lesson".
   - **Crafty** was scaffolding to make up for weak models, and became useless as models improved.
   - **Blackberry** had you pre-write commit messages as a plan. Models learned to plan themselves, and humans don't pre-plan commits; design docs won instead.
   - **Sculptor** is what survived: an open-source, model-agnostic, parallel-agent IDE with one container per agent, "pairing mode" sync into your local checkout, and a heavily customizable UI. The pitch is "the one thing big labs won't build": a tool designed to keep you *off* their platform.
   - **Bounty ideas:** fall back from a Claude Max plan to an open model when you hit the limit; a "nightshift" mode.
   
   ([Imbue](https://imbue.com/blog/our-lessons-from-building-an-open-source-coding-agent) · [GitHub](https://github.com/imbue-ai/sculptor))

5. **Latent Space Lightning, "Anthropic: Know Before You IPO" with Tanay Jaipuria (Wing VC)** (Nov 18, 53-min podcast). Standing radar (Latent Space). A pre-debut walk through the S-1: revenue mix, compute costs and inference margins, distribution through cloud partners, and "why now". The best single listen if you want to understand the ANT numbers before forming a view. ([Latent Space podcasts](https://latent.space/podcasts/))

6. **Lauren Tan (@poteto), "Automate Anything with Grok Bot"** (Nov 18, guide). Standing radar (agentic engineering practice), and trending on X today.
   - **Start from the outcome**, not the steps.
   - **Voice-dump how you do the task today.** Have the bot restate it and interview you about the decisions you skipped.
   - **Then wire it up:** connect the needed apps and feedback sources, and add a trigger or schedule.
   
   The interview step is the transferable trick for any agent onboarding flow. ([X summary](https://x.com/i/trending/2123121150431551865) · [The Shiplog](https://shiplog.news/p/2123159537016533493))
