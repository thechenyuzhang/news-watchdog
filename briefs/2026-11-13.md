## What to read / what to watch

**This week's theme:** reading launch claims carefully, and building with several models instead of one.

1. **Gautam Parab, "Five Times Cheaper Than Google's Own Model"** (Nov 12, essay). **New creator.**
   - Reads Google's Gemini Decision 1 benchmark table row by row. "Nearly 5x cheaper" is versus Google's own Flash‑Lite, not other decision models: in Google's own cost row **Jev is cheapest ($0.68 vs $0.88)**, and Decision 1 beats Jev on 5 of 7 shared evals, mostly thanks to one task. Jev wins When2Call (tool calling), which is the first use Google lists.
   - Explains why Google's calibration error (half of Jev's) and Artificial Analysis's Brier score (nearly tied) can both be right. Practical advice: run When2Call-style items from your own traffic through Decision 1, Jev and Drex Max before trusting any launch table.
   ([gautamparab.com](https://gautamparab.com/writing/five-times-cheaper-than-googles-own-model/))

2. **OpenAI, "Cyber at the Frontier" keynote** (Nov 12, video, ~1h). Greg Brockman on the PHP-shell case (a researcher found it with OpenAI's model; a month later the next model found a more general version on its own; disclosure took two months of coordination with governments), then Nancy Wang walks through Daybreak Red Team and Defense Factory. Worth it if you build security or devtools. ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YmxpxJZvm4M))

3. **Google Antigravity, "Gemini 4 Hydrogen and Orchestrator"** + **Simon Willison on Hydrogen pricing** (Nov 12). Google explains why static routers fail on long agent tasks and how Orchestrator switches models mid-trajectory. ([Antigravity](https://antigravity.google/blog/gemini-4-hydrogen-in-google-antigravity/) · [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/12/))

4. **Latent Space AINews, "D-Day (Decision Models Day)"** (Nov 11). ([Latent Space](https://lspace.beehiiv.com/p/ainews-d-day-decision-models-day))
