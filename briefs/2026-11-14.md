## What to read / what to watch

**This week's theme:** how fast is the frontier really moving, and what's left for humans and specialist models.

1. **Dwarkesh Patel, "The avalanche that hit math is coming for AI development"** (Nov 13, essay + narrated video). *Standing radar.*
   - Argues AI research is verifiable enough (pretraining loss, RL sample efficiency, inference metrics, internal benchmarks like Anthropic's CoBench) to be automated the way well-scoped math was. Median: AI as good as the best human AI researchers by **2028**, 25% chance it slips past 2032, and 2027 possible.
   - After that: 50/50 between a "software-only singularity" and a "spark" (3–6 years of progress in one year, then compute-bound). Cites METR (Anthropic's rate of progress on well-scoped tasks doubled, mostly from AI-assisted R&D) and OpenAI's heaviest internal users going from ~$1K to $7K+/day on coding agents.
   - Read the Appendix "objections" section (continual learning, data, experiment compute). It's the most useful part for founders planning 2027.
   ([dwarkesh.com](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi))

2. **Haddy Alchaer, "After 600 experiments, here's what I discovered about JEPA world models"** (Nov 2026, interactive essay; HN 273). **New creator.**
   - On LeCun's LeWM (Push-T), swapping the learned predictor for perfect simulator physics changed almost nothing (78% vs 77%). The gains came from planning: chaining 25-step subgoals took long-horizon success from **22% to 80–85%**, with no retraining.
   - A latent memory graph of demonstrations with a cost per cross-demo jump matched expert subgoals. The standard benchmark is easier than it looks (84% of test goals were already near stored states; seed noise flipped 38/250 episodes). And dropping the pusher from the goal cost took the exact-physics planner from 9/50 to 39/50. Lesson for robotics founders: define goals as sets of end states, not images.
   ([haddyalchaer.com](https://haddyalchaer.com/jepa))

3. **Anthropic, "Building strong safety evaluations"** (Nov 13, guide). Psychology-style rigor for evals: expert-built scenarios, sandboxed runs, multi-turn tests scored on "appropriate response rate," validating LLM graders against humans, preregistered metrics, ≥100 items for complex multi-turn and ≥1,000 for single-turn, and reporting uncertainty. Candid: single-turn harmlessness is saturated (94–98%), and a pre-launch transcript review caught Claude implicitly validating self-harm (fixed from 60% to 94% via system prompt). Use it as a checklist for your own product evals. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-strong-safety-evaluations))

4. **Deepnote, Agent Initiative Benchmark** (Nov 13, open benchmark + dataset). **New creator (institutional research).** 12 models dropped into a synthetic company (PageMantle: Slack, CRM, billing, warehouse, tracker) across 50 situations, scored on whether they gather evidence, decide to help, and pick the right recipient. GPT‑6 Astra 8.97 (95% CI 8.30–9.52, $0.69/task), GPT‑6.1 Sol 8.65, Claude Fable 5.2 8.17, Grok 4.7 last at 4.51. Common failure: not staying quiet. ([Deepnote](https://deepnote.com/research/benchmark) · [GitHub](https://github.com/deepnote/agent-initiative-benchmark))

5. **OpenAI, Commerce Partner Playbook** (with ChatGPT Wallet, Nov 13, primary doc). The practical checklist for making a store agent-purchasable: product feeds, Web Bot Auth, agent-aware risk rules, plugins/WebMCP tools. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet/))
