## What to read / what to watch

**Today's theme:** what it costs to run AI products and agent fleets honestly: perf regressions, fraud, verification, and the skills you lose.

1. **OpenAI (Okay Zed, Cyrus Forbes), "How OpenAI's Performance Factory keeps ChatGPT fast"** (Nov 19, engineering post). Standing radar (lab blogs).
   - **The system.** When a perf alert fires, a Codex agent opens a task, investigates, records findings and proposes a fix for an engineer to review. After deploy, a *second* agent checks that latency is back to baseline.
   - **The numbers (OpenAI's).** 1,700+ investigations and 600+ root-caused in just over three months, about 17 a day.
   - **The transferable part.** They encoded 30+ internal perf tools and the team's playbook into one `perf-forensics` skill (find onset, find affected requests, diff deploys/config/flags against metrics, traces and profiles), then turned it into a workflow.
   - **Next:** a "rightsizing" skill for over-provisioned autoscaling; the current bottleneck is human review and merge.
   
   A clean template for any ops-agent product: alert → investigate → propose → verify. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-openai-performance-factory-keeps-chatgpt-fast))

2. **Claire Vo, "How I fight token fraud as a solo founder"** (Nov 16 episode of How I AI / Lenny's Newsletter, ~40 min; amplified by Willison on Nov 20). **New creator** to these briefs (via the Lenny's standing feed). She lost tens of thousands of dollars to:
   - farmed trials;
   - resold promo codes;
   - relay wrappers;
   - a custom MCP that ran as a 24/7 compute worker. One ring ran Roblox/Fortnite map production through her agent.

   Her defences:
   - business controls, then Stripe Radar, Vercel BotID/Firewall and Clerk;
   - **a Jev loop classifying abuse in real time**;
   - treating the AI support desk as an attack vector.

   She ended free trials for good. Willison adds that the scammers' pelican-SVG prompts are probably model fingerprinting. ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-i-fight-token-fraud-as-a-solo) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZDpP7JXPlhc) · [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/))

3. **Jackson Ly, "What broke when I ran 20 coding agents at 229B tokens a month"** (Nov 12, essay). **New creator**, first time in these briefs.
   - **The failures.** One agent ran 4.5 days on 40.8M tokens with zero commits. "CI green" after 28 of 308 tests. A prod outage while he slept.
   - **Model split.** A frontier model plans and reviews; cheaper GLM/Grok/DeepSeek models execute. That halved Claude spend per task.
   - **Briefs as contracts.** Interfaces, invariants and **failing acceptance tests written first**; a QA gate, not the agent's report, decides "done". False-"done" runs fell from 36% to 11%.
   - **Ops.** A rule file that grew from 434 to 1,554 lines in a day *reduced* compliance. CI went from ~60 to <10 minutes per agent (~$120/month). No production merges while asleep. Keep every transcript in a ledger.
   
   He open-sourced keryx and Morndo. ([jacksonly.xyz](https://jacksonly.xyz/blog/what-broke-running-20-coding-agents))

4. **Tim Genewein (Google DeepMind), "What comes after AGI?"** (Nov 19, essay plus technical report with Legg, Dafoe, Hutter et al.). Standing radar (DeepMind Institute).
   - **ASI defined** as consistently beating large groups of experts on years-long tasks, with fewer resources.
   - **"Cognitive industrialization":** an X summary cites ~10x/year growth in effective compute.
   - **Four pathways:** scaling models and data, paradigm shifts, agent coordination, and **RSI**.
   - **The ask:** "model, measure, forecast, repeat" with frontier-model access and evaluation compute, which may "spawn entirely new research fields".
   
   It pairs with Newsom's RSI-cap recommendation and OpenAI's jagged-frontier report as this week's RSI-measurement trilogy. ([DeepMind Institute](https://institute.deepmind.com/essays/what-comes-after-agi/) · [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50569519))

5. **Pete Warden with Ben Lorica, "Why AI Wants to Be Local"** (Nov 19, podcast plus transcript; HN **264**/238 comments). **New creator** to these briefs (Warden; Moonshine Voice / Useful Sensors).
   - **Why local stays under-marketed:** nobody can bill for it, and the tooling lags.

   - **Hardware:** "using an oil tanker to go and do your shopping" is his line on GPUs for inference. Inference is memory-bound, so high-bandwidth flash and Apple-style RAM bandwidth matter more than FLOPs.
