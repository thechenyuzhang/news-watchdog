## What to read / what to watch

**Today's theme:** how close self-improvement is, and the unglamorous engineering (harnesses, gates, deletion) that decides whether you benefit from it or get hurt by it.

1. **Dwarkesh Podcast, "Paul Christiano – We could be 12 months away from AIs that can overthrow the government"** (Nov 16, podcast; standing radar). The most-discussed safety interview of the week on X.
   - Christiano (an OpenAI Foundation board member and alignment pioneer) gives **~1-in-3 odds of superintelligence within a year** on current trajectories with no slowdown, and puts the risk of AI disempowering humanity over the next 6–12 months **"closer to 10% than 1%"**.
   - His concrete ask is narrower than "stop AI": **pause algorithmic progress in pre-training and RL** until labs can secure weights and research secrets against theft and can reliably assess control.
   
   Pair it with the GPT‑6.2 system card the same week. ([Dwarkesh](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/paul-christiano-2))

2. **Kernel, "Why is Gemini 4 Carbon taking so long?"** (Nov 2026, eval writeup; HN 202). **New creator.** A trace-level teardown of why Carbon spent close to an hour on bug-fix tasks that Opus and Astra finished in minutes.
   - Slower inference explains only 16–30% of the gap against Astra (about half against Opus). The rest is behaviour: Carbon took **~7x the steps and 11x the actions**, processed 16x the input, and spent about half its steps auditing code beyond the bug or closing loopholes the ticket had ruled out.
   - **39% of the lines it read were re-reads** (against 7% for Astra).
   - An injected check-in every five turns ("do you already have enough to make the fix?") cut a 37-minute run to 28.
   
   Steal that check-in trick for any over-thorough model. ([kernel.dev](https://kernel.dev/blog/why-is-gemini-4-carbon-taking-so-long/))

3. **Runta (Shilin Zhu) with Baseten, "Introducing FrontierHarness Eval v1.1"** (Nov 17, benchmark; HN 410). **New creator.** 22 harnesses × 5 open models × 62 terminal tasks × 4 attempts, which is 27,280 runs and 83B tokens.
   - **The best-value harness changes with the model.** Codex CLI went from 1st on GLM-5.3 to 16th on Kimi-K3, and the median harness moved 11 of 22 places by cost.
   - The model explains 92.8% of cost variance, but with the model fixed, harnesses still differ **1.9–4.9x in cost per pass**.
   - Higher cost doesn't buy pass rate. **64.5% of spend went to failed runs**, mostly ending in a confident wrong answer.
   - For harness builders: cut *steps*, not prompt tokens.
   
   ([runta.com](https://runta.com/blog/introducing-frontierharness-eval-v1-1/))

4. **PostHog (Jina Yoon), "Not everything needs an LLM: how we use decision models"** (Nov 9, newly on HN at 169; first time in these briefs). A production playbook for Jev-style decision models:
   - **Gate expensive calls.** Two narrow yes/no PII questions let **98.7%** of session summaries skip Gemini entirely.
   - **Set thresholds by error cost.** 0.4 where a miss leaks PII; 0.85 where false positives annoy users in Slack.
   - **Route** with one `choice` question ("cheapest option that will most likely finish correctly on the first try").
   - **Rerank** embedding results, which gave ~25% better ranking.
   - Don't chain decision models for multi-step reasoning. Thresholds don't carry across model versions, so keep judges in shadow mode while you recalibrate.
   
   ([PostHog newsletter](https://newsletter.posthog.com/p/decision-models))

5. **a16z "Working Theory" ep. 1 (Sarah Wang with Mark Chen, OpenAI, and John Schulman, Thinking Machines), "Can AI learn to improve itself?"** (this week, video podcast). Fresh institutional piece.
   - Both define RSI as **AI accelerating AI research** (pre-training, RL, experiments, evals), not models autonomously building models.
   - OpenAI says it already has "AI research interns", measured by the compute Codex manages end to end for experiments.
   - Chen puts OpenAI at "maybe 80%" on the AGI spectrum but "25–50%" on RSI. Research taste is the gap.
   - Schulman argues for alignment as a "public science" done widely, rather than overfit to a couple of companies.
   
   ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_aA1-R70E8))

6. **Calif (Quang Luong), "Java4Shell: Write once, RCE everywhere"** (Nov 17, disclosure plus essay). *3rd high-signal hit in these briefs (PHP2Shell, Oct 21; iPhone jailbreak, Oct 25), so Calif is promoted to the standing radar today.* Beyond the bug (TLDR #4):
   - **How it was found.** Luong "told Codex to look at" JIT, intrinsics and crypto paths, sifted the slop, and kept prompting. Most later exploit chains were built almost autonomously.
   - **The essay's thesis.** Bloat is the attack surface. Use the same "centaur hacker" setup for **"vibe-uncoding"**: find and delete the features and hand-rolled optimizations you don't need.
   
   ([calif.io](https://calif.io/java4shell/))
