## TLDR

1. **OpenAI's cyber agents found and exploited 948 Linux local privilege-escalation bugs in one run — $1.21M in API credits (~$1,279 per working LPE) (Confirmed, Nov 4).**
   - Pipeline (upcoming "cyber model"): Scout → Analyst → dynamic validation → ExploitDev → patches. 2,308 candidates → 1,724 reproduced → 948 distinct LPE exploits on a stock desktop distro with SMEP/SMAP/KASLR on; also 291 info-leak PoCs. On hardened kernelCTF-like targets: 21 LPEs for ~$33K.

## What to read / what to watch

**This week's theme:** harnesses, pricing power, and what "done" means when agents write the code.

1. **Vadim Kokovin, "Harness Engineering: a Reliable AI Agent in Six Layers"** (Nov 5, ~25 min essay, **New creator**).
   - Clean map: task contract → context compiler → permissioned tool gateway → durable state → evidence gates → trace/recovery. Metric that matters: *accepted outputs per human review minute*.
   - Maps the same layers onto Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Copilot, Gemini CLI and custom agents; argues the harness is capital and the model is swappable. Six-part series (one layer/day).
   - Why now: pairs directly with OpenAI's cyber-post note that stronger models need *lighter* orchestration, and with Ask HN's agentic-setup thread.

   ([kokovin.su](https://kokovin.su/en/articles/harness-engineering-reliable-ai-agent-six-layers/))

2. **Tugce Erten (a16z), "How AI Applications Earn Their Premium"** (Nov 2, institutional essay).
   - Three rules: operate the workflow better than the customer can; make context cheaper to rent than to rebuild; prove the value, then price the unit of work (not tokens).
   - Worked examples: Harvey vs Copilot/Lexia at Repsol; Decagon at Notion; Clay's credit split for volatile reasoning. Build-vs-buy as a continuous test as models improve.

   ([a16z](https://a16z.com/how-ai-applications-earn-their-premium/))

3. **OpenAI, "A thousand fewer paths to root"** (Nov 4, long research post — also TLDR #1).
   - Read as an *ops* essay: scout/analyst/exploit/patch pipeline costs, false-path exploits, and the "rescan when models improve" doctrine. Best current primary on what frontier cyber agents actually do at scale.

   ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/a-thousand-fewer-paths-to-root/))

4. **Holdover (still high-signal):** Neil Kakkar in The Pragmatic Engineer, "How software engineering is changing: a tale of two eras" (Nov 3) — 200+ PRs/month, CI review agent, revert rate down. ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-software-engineering-is-changing))
