## TLDR

1. **Your prompts may not be as private as your provider says: Dragos caught attackers inside three AI inference providers, and OpenRouter acted on it today (Confirmed).**
   - **The study.** HD Moore's team at Dragos sent ~2 million prompts carrying unique canary URLs and real-but-empty AWS keys through AI providers and routers from July to November. Seeded secrets were used against Dragos's test systems, which shows that data from **at least three providers** was exposed. In two of those breaches some stolen requests were marked **zero data retention (ZDR)**.
   - **Beyond breaches.** Resellers on an inference marketplace could read and keep customer prompts with ordinary logging. Some providers ignored privacy settings: prompts sent to US providers reached Chinese AI organizations, and one seeded link turned up in an internal company chat in China. Dragos names no providers.
   - **OpenRouter today (Nov 22).** It **off-boarded three providers**, pulled **125 endpoints from ZDR eligibility** and notified affected customers. It is adapting Dragos's method into ongoing checks. It links provider disclosures from **Together** ("prompt leakage for requests in June and July of 2026"), **Novita** and **Phala**. OpenRouter says it was not itself breached. HN 224.
   - **Do this week:** pin upstream providers instead of the default pool, stay off reseller marketplaces, and put a canary credential in your agent context. Dragos's own advice; see K #1.
   ([Dragos](https://www.dragos.com/blog/ai-supply-chain-security-canary-tokens) · [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/data-processing-incidents-on-inference-providers/) · [HN front page](https://news.ycombinator.com/))

2. **Washington moves from chips to models: a House report urges pulling Chinese open-weight models out of federal systems, and Cursor says it will "explore phasing out" PRC models (Confirmed).**
   - **The report** (Homeland Security chair Garbarino and China Select chair Moolenaar, Nov 20). It says PRC-developed models should be removed from federal systems and from high-risk critical-infrastructure functions, and federal contractors shouldn't use them for federal work. Exceptions would need DHS/CISA approval after a **CAISSI** evaluation. It also proposes a national American open-weight initiative and a model-provenance standard.
   - **The case studies.** Airbnb runs customized **Qwen** models for support (~80,000 interactions a day). Cursor's **Composer 2** is built on Moonshot's **Kimi K2.5**. DoorDash had authorized **25 PRC model families**. Per CNBC, Cursor told the committees it plans to "explore phasing out PRC-developed open-weight models because of geopolitical risk". There's no timeline and no replacement named.
   - **Same week:** Trump threatened a "tremendous tax" on US companies that build data centers abroad, giving no details (The Verge).
   - **The counterpoint (K #2):** Sebastian Raschka's Saturday post argues US and Chinese open-weight models have each been built on the other's ideas.
   ([House report PDF](https://homeland.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/2026.11.20-Joint-Report-PRC-AI-Models.pdf) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/cursor-chinese-ai-models-geopolitical-risk.html) · [AI Pro Playbook: Trump tax](https://aiproplaybook.com/top-ai-stories/2026-11-21))

3. **RH: independent verification is nearly finished, and the human proof is in trouble (Confirmed / Likely).**

It also shows **con-ron** (a Rust port of con-leche) confirming all five theorems against the challenge statement, and every module replayed through `leanchecker`. Only two checks are still "running": Lean's default and paranoid kernel replays of the export, and the end-to-end `lake comparator` run.
   - **Masullo's 940-page human GRH draft.** A note circulating Saturday cites J.L. Nielsen's counterexample to a key step: a uniform-boundedness claim that fails because certain integrals diverge. A public ERRATA file lists further gaps (Likely, not yet acknowledged by Masullo or Conrad).

   ([Sutherland](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) · [Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/ai-may-have-solved-math-s-greatest-mystery-can-humans-understand-answer) · [ERRATA](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/blob/main/ERRATA.md) · [Nielsen note via De Domenico](https://substack.com/@manlius/note/c-393460806))

## What to read / what to watch

**Today's theme:** the trust layer: what you can verify about where your prompts go, which model you run, and what your agents shipped.

1. **HD Moore (Dragos), "Three breaches, endless leaks: Your AI supply chain needs canaries"** (Nov 19, 15-min read). **New creator** for this list (Metasploit's creator, now Dragos Chief Research Officer); found through Layer 2 security queries.
   - **Method.** Unique URLs, DNS names and real AWS keys tied to empty, fully logged accounts. Any use proves someone saw the prompt, and each value maps to the exact model, router and privacy setting. Dragos traced ~2,000 routes; several hundred passed through a system it couldn't identify.
   - **Findings.** One attacker appears to have compromised two providers at once. Its tooling evolved over three weeks, and on Nov 1 it added an LLM-driven pentest framework (ARTEX). A key seeded Oct 21 was used the next day and was still in use three weeks later.
   - **Five fixes worth copying.** Fewer hops; buy first-tier; pick upstreams yourself; seed your own canaries (a free Thinkst Canarytoken is enough); turn on secret filtering.
   ([Dragos](https://www.dragos.com/blog/ai-supply-chain-security-canary-tokens))

2. **Sebastian Raschka, "The US-China Open-Weight Feedback Loop"** (Nov 21, short post plus figure). Standing radar.
   - **The claim.** "US open-weight models wouldn't be this good without Chinese open-weight models and vice versa," and proprietary models wouldn't be this good or cheap without open weights.
   - **The receipts table.** LLaMA took RoPE from RoFormer; DeepSeek LLM took Google's GQA; OLMoE took DeepSeekMoE's fine-grained experts; DeepSeek-V3 took Meta's multi-token prediction; Kimi K2 used MLA; Arcee's Trinity uses Qwen's gated attention; AMD's Instella combines MLA with output gating.
   - **Why read it today.** It's the cleanest technical counterweight to the House report: provenance policy will have to separate *ideas* from *weights*.
   ([sebastianraschka.com](https://sebastianraschka.com/blog/2026/us-china-open-weight-feedback-loop.html))

3. **Ankit Jain, "If we stop reading the code, what do we read?"** (The New Stack, Nov 21; built on his Hangar DX podcast interviews). **New creator.**
   - **Uncle Bob's review tool.** Robert Martin says he never sees 95% of his agents' code and reviews a live, UML-like map instead. It flags high CRAP scores (complexity × poor test coverage), low mutation-testing scores and dependency arrows that point the wrong way. "The old dream of UML… now a dynamic thing that you do after the code is produced."
   - **The skeptic.** Ray Myers: most "don't read code" people are "doing some lowercase form of actually reading the code."
   - **GitHub's view.** David Poll, who leads pull requests and code review at GitHub: the question is whether attention is pointed "where it's actually useful."
   - **Jain's prescription.** Hand reviewers intent, criteria backed by evidence, and decisions recorded where they were made. Ownership never moves: "The agents are not responsible for anything."
   ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/ai-agent-code-review/))

4. **Zach Lloyd (Warp), "Software Engineering Is Becoming Factory Engineering"** (talk on YouTube; upload date not verified, flagged as a new talk in Saturday digests). **New creator.**
   - **The loop.** Ideas → agent triage → spec (human review) → agent implementation → human and agent code review → agent verification → human product review → ship → monitor. Engineers become the people who build and run that factory.
   - **Datapoints he gives.** Warp has 60K+ GitHub stars and 800K+ active developers. Lloyd hasn't written a line of code in six months, and Warp is "hiring more people than we've ever hired".
   - **The caveat he adds himself.** Product taste is the part you can't automate.
   ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tUPPVhBBcoM) · [Warp Factories post](https://www.warp.dev/blog/open-infrastructure-for-building-a-software-factory))

5. **Graham Dumpleton, the Muru build log** (Nov 4–22, 19 posts), found one hop from Simon Willison's Nov 21 link. **New creator.**

   - **The line to keep**, from "AI as a lifestyle choice": "deciding what isn't good enough yet is still down to me, and twenty years of getting things wrong with mod_wsgi turns out to be useful for that."
