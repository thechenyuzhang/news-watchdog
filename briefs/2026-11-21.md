## TLDR

1. **The Riemann Hypothesis now has a machine-checked proof: the Lean and Mathlib communities certified Alpöge's Claude-built proof on Saturday morning (Confirmed).**
   - **The certification.** A joint statement by the Lean and Mathlib communities, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, says: "We have full confidence that Alpöge's proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct." All four of Kevin Buzzard's criteria are met: the statement is Mathlib's own `RiemannHypothesis`; dozens of people reproduced the build; `comparator` and `con-leche --compare` both match the proved theorem to Mathlib's statement; and several independent proof checkers accept the exported proof. The checks ran on an inert export, out of reach of any malicious code in the sources.
   - **What was proved.** About 17 million lines of Lean in 175,187 modules. There are five headline theorems: RH, plus **GRH for all Dirichlet L-functions** (all moduli and characters). The only axioms used are Lean's standard three (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`). Out of scope: Dedekind zeta, Hecke and general automorphic L-functions. Combined with an independent formalization of Robin's criterion, it also yields Robin's and Lagarias's inequalities with no mention of zeta.
   - **Still pending (as of 4:55 AM PST).** Sutherland's MIT check page lists the end-to-end `lake comparator` run and Lean's default and paranoid kernel replays of the export as "running". The independent nanoda and con-leche kernels have already accepted it, and lean4lean passed every module. Anthropic has not commented (Nature, BBC).
   - **The human proof, same day.** Stanford's Ravi Vakil rushed out Alessandro Masullo's **940-page, "all human, with no AI"** GRH draft, which Brian Conrad had been reviewing for months. About 8 hours later Vakil wrote that several people had reported problems in it, "identified with the assistance of different AI models."
   - **Physics too.** Terence Tao lists a human-readable, Lean-formalized proof of **finite-time blow-up for the unforced 3D Euler equations** (Alpöge, Buckmaster, Coiculescu), and a Lean-formalized unforced Navier–Stokes blow-up that still needs a professional write-up.
   - **The conversation.** HN: "Alpöge claims proof of GRH" **2,759 points and 1,933 comments**; Vakil's post 2,257; Tao 490; Scott Aaronson 320 ("my bet is that the proof will stand"); Borretti's dissent "Re: Riemann" 205.
   - **Why founders care.** "AI result + formal certificate + independent kernels" just became the gold standard for a hard claim. Verification tooling (Lean infrastructure, statement comparison, independent kernels) is now a real product category; &Truth, Harmonic, Theorem and Axiom are the named players (K #2).

5. **Patch: GPGME CVE-2026-129975, a double free with RCE "not ruled out" (Confirmed).**
   - **The bug.** An OpenPGP key with a crafted, very long user ID triggers a double free in GPGME's status-line reader. That can exfiltrate data, and RCE can't be ruled out.
   - **Who's affected.** Most mail clients, many XMPP clients, Samba, Flatpak, Gpg4win and GnuPG Desktop. Update to **GPGME 2.3.1** (or Gpg4win 5.1.2), and rebuild static binaries.
   - **Timing.** Disclosure moved up from Nov 25 because GitLab had to patch early for **Ruby2ßhell**. HN 120.
   - **Still pending:** the OpenSSL CVE-2026-121481 advisory.

## What to read / what to watch

**Today's theme:** what counts as proof now, and how much of it a human has to read.

1. **swyx / Latent Space AINews, "Riemann Hypothesis proved...twice independently"** (Nov 20, ~24-min newsletter). Standing radar (Latent Space).
   - **A timeline of both claims.** Alpöge's Lean-only proof (~1.3GB, about a 250,000-page equivalent) and Masullo's 940-page human draft, 12 years in the making.
   - **The contrast he draws.** The AI proof is "probably correct but incomprehensible"; the human one is "probably technically incorrect but understandable."
   - **Two framings worth stealing.** Alpöge chose Lean-only *to avoid releasing "slop"*, which tests the No-Slop Manifesto's "understand what you ship." And this is **weak-to-strong** collaboration in practice: Claude left its human collaborator behind on the hardest automorphic-forms steps.
   ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-riemann-hypothesis-proved-twice-independently/))

2. **Richard MacManus / Latent Space, "The War Against Slop and an Emerging Weapon to Fight it: Formal Verification"** (Nov 20, essay). Fresh institutional piece.
   - **The players.** A field guide to &Truth (Clark Barrett: cvc5 is called "billions of times a day" at Amazon), Harmonic's Aristotle, Axiom's AxiomProver/AXLE, and Theorem.
   - **The key distinction.** Testing, patching and scanning find a lot, but "you're never quite sure" you found everything.
   - **Data points.** Anthropic and Theorem report Opus 5.5 reaching a 50% solve rate on 36 verification tasks with ~40% fewer tokens than Opus 5.
   - **"Veri-coding."** Developers spend their time on specs and review; the agent works with the prover underneath.
   ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/formal-verification/))

3. **Alex L. Zhang, Souradip Chakraborty, Omar Khattab (MIT CSAIL), "Language Model Harnesses Can Be Compositional Generalizers"** (paper submitted Nov 20; #1 trending on alphaXiv; companion blog). **Repeat creator, promoted to the standing radar today.** Zhang and Khattab were co-authors of "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming" (HN 2,877, Nov 2 brief).
   - **The claim.** A harness's job is to keep every LM call "locally in-distribution."
   - **The evidence.** RL on a Recursive Language Model harness trained on short tasks generalizes to tasks **2–64× longer** and transfers across domains with shared structure.
   - **"Surrogate harness training."** Train individual sub-calls on synthetic environments with fake tools. RLM-27B improves Qwen3.8-27B by **26.9%** on hard long-context suites with ~2× fewer tokens.
   - **Takeaway for agent builders:** put the effort into harness decomposition before more fine-tuning.
   ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.harness-cg) · [Blog](https://alexzhang13.github.io/blog/2026/harness/))

4. **Thinking Machines, "Introducing Inkling 2 Mini"** (Nov 20, model post with a long research section). Standing radar (lab blog).
   - **Read past the benchmarks to the monitorability section.** A no-CoT time horizon goes from 16s to 37s from one to three loops. Serial-reasoning gains continue to 10 loops, twice what it was trained with. CoT gets **less than half as long but stays legible**.
   - **Monitor results.** On LinuxArena, a weaker monitor misses many attacks when it sees only tool calls, with no clear trend by loop count.
   - The clearest public data on whether latent reasoning breaks CoT oversight.
   ([TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/))

5. **Mani Doraisamy, "We promoted developers into architects without informing them"** (Nov 20, essay; HN **507**). **New creator** to these briefs.

   - **The thesis.** AI implements requirements and optimizes for security and backward compatibility, but it never pushes back on a requirement. So someone has to own architecture: decide the data model first, constrain the agent to it, and review like an architect.
