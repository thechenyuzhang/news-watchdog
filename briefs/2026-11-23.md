4. **Zhipu shipped GLM-5.4, a 750B "Loop Transformer" that reuses its middle layers twice instead of adding parameters (Confirmed, HN 536).**
   - **How.** 28 layers of the GLM-5 base run for two passes. Zhipu converted an existing pretrained base model through continued training instead of starting from scratch. Per-token gates control how much each pass writes back. The second pass reuses the first pass's KV cache and sparse-attention indices, so an extra loop costs only a sliding window of KV at 1M context.
   - **Claims (not independently checked).** "Comparable to 2T-3T parameter models". In a 30B ablation, ~10% more compute gave clear gains, with outputs 10–14% shorter. Speed is 200 tok/s off-peak and 70–80 at peak. Zhipu admits the model can get stuck in "marathon thinking" loops.
   - **Availability.** Live on the GLM Coding Plan, ZCode and Z.ai Chat. The API is "coming soon", and open weights are planned "after completing necessary safety hardening". Zhipu says a "significantly larger model" will be "ready soon".
   - **Why it matters.** It's the second middle-layer-reuse model in a week, after Inkling 2 Mini. With the House report and Cursor's phase-out, though, a Chinese base model is now a procurement question for US buyers.
   ([Z.ai](https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.4) · [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50609685))

## What to read / what to watch

**Today's theme:** how fast is this going, and how would we know? Two institutional measurements, two outside readers on the RH proof, and one practical exit from the cloud.

1. **Jakub Pachocki (OpenAI Chief Scientist), "Perplexity and Intelligence"** (Nov 22, ~10-min read). Fresh institutional piece, and the most-discussed lab post on HN this weekend (640).
   - **The argument.** Modern AI mostly harnesses "a much simpler and less interpretable process: training a compressor… on a very large corpus." Benchmarks saturate, but pretraining loss "is likely to stay informative".
   - **The numbers OpenAI had never published.** Log-perplexity was ~0.99 at GPT-3.5, ~0.91 at GPT-4, below 0.8 "transformative capabilities" in cyber and math, and ~0.76 for the model whose math results arrived "one after another in a single Slack thread". The plot shows "a steep acceleration in 2026", mostly from long-term research and compute and "only in a smaller part" from automation.
   - **The proposal.** Shared evaluation norms, red-teamed safety cases "by the time they are first used", and common limits labs won't cross until monitoring hits a milestone.
   - **His own caveats.** Datasets differ across labs, perplexity isn't a task, chain-of-thought gains don't show up in it, and the floor is unknown.
   ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/) · [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50601525))

2. **METR, "AI R&D-relevant model capabilities are improving rapidly and are getting harder to measure"** (Nov 20, long report). Fresh institutional piece; not covered in earlier briefs.
   - **Headline.** Public evidence "is insufficient to rule out drastic AI R&D acceleration in the next couple of years", meaning a pace at which humans can no longer "meaningfully understand, assess, and react" to catastrophic risks.
   - **Findings.**
     - Models usually match or beat skilled humans on METR's coding and optimization tasks; METR "typically failed to find humans that outperform the current frontier models".
     - Combined time horizons double about every **3.6 months** (95% CI 2.1–4.9). That's similar to what OpenAI reported on its internal research tasks.
     - Agents show worse judgment than experts when feedback is slow, and **model judges overrate outputs from equally strong or stronger models**.
     - Tasks that would be costly to turn into RL environments are harder for models. As a rough guide, doubling that cost has about the same effect as halving the time horizon.
   - **Founder use:** if you use LLM-as-judge for evals, the judge-bias finding is a direct warning. Keep a human or a weaker-model audit in the loop for your hardest outputs.
   ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/) · [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/20/1149399/the-most-misunderstood-graph-in-ai-just-got-a-major-update/))

3. **micjm, "Yes, 17m lines of Lean is a lot"** (Nov 22, 3-min read, HN 186, 226 comments). **New creator.** Found by a Layer 2 query on Lean and the RH proof.
   - **Method.** The author charts proof lengths from Mathlib's 259 formalized famous theorems and from the 938 Lean packages on Reservoir that have code on GitHub.
   - **Findings.** No human-written famous proof comes close. Only a few AI-built projects exceed 16M lines: a work-in-progress CFSG proof at 88M, LeanBridge at 36M (but ~98% script-generated checks), and Anthropic's AI-built Fermat's Last Theorem proof at 27M, "roughly 450 times longer" than the ~60K-line human FLT project.
   - **Why read it:** it sizes the "proofs no human can read" problem with data instead of adjectives, which is useful context for Maynard's and Radziwill's understanding and credit critique.
   ([micjm](https://micjm.com/blog/yes-17m-lines-of-lean-is-a-lot/) · [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50607668))

4. **John Baez, "Masullo on the Riemann Hypothesis"** (Azimuth, Nov 22, long post, HN 267). **New creator** for this list.
   - **What it is.** The first plain-language walk-through of Masullo's 940-page human draft. Baez notes that both Nov 20 attempts actually target the *generalized* RH.
   - **Masullo's strategy.** A new "trace formula" for the logarithmic derivative of zeta that visibly has no poles to the right of the critical line, which is what RH requires. The post builds up from the explicit formula ("the music of the primes").
   - **Then the problems.** Baez walks through the gaps readers have flagged, and says up front that he isn't a number theorist and the account is "very tentative".
   ([Azimuth](https://johncarlosbaez.wordpress.com/2026/11/22/masullo-on-the-riemann-hypothesis/))

5. **Jerry James (GPU Partner), "A beginner's guide to Local AI with Inkling, Qwen, and Unsloth"** (Nov 22, 10-min read, **HN 632**). **New creator.**
   - **The recipe.** Install Unsloth Desktop, pick a quantized model that fits your VRAM, set the context size, and turn on LAN access so the whole house can use it. It takes "about 10-15 minutes (excluding download times)".
   - **The VRAM table is the useful part.** 8–12GB runs Qwen3.5-9B or Gemma 4 12B at Q4. 16GB runs MoE models (Qwen3.6-35B at ~47 tok/s on an RTX 5060 Ti, per their calculator; Inkling 2 Mini). 24–32GB runs Qwen3.8-27B (Q4 with 128K context fits a 5090).
   - **Caveat:** GPU Partner sells GPUs, so read the hardware advice with that in mind.
   ([GPU Partner](https://www.gpupartner.com/blog/beginners-guide-local-ai-inkling-qwen-unsloth) · [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50603845))
